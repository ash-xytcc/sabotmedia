## What the article does

The article embeds a graphic matching Sabot Media’s A/I campaign graphic headed “WHAT A/I CAN SURVIVE,” with the paired “BUILT FOR / TARGETED NOW” layout and the line:

> PLAN R* CAN REPLACE A MACHINE. IT CANNOT REPLACE THE WORLD AROUND IT.

In the text immediately surrounding the image, Shideler does not identify Sabot Media. Instead, he describes the source generically as a “recent post” circulating on Instagram among “Antifa and anarchist accounts.” He then paraphrases the graphic’s point that sanctions target “the world around” A/I and reproduces the substance of Sabot’s legal-caution material, including the warning not to improvise donations, reroute funds, disguise payments, access private data, promise direct services, or guess at sanctions law.

Sabot’s underlying article, “The Server Called Paranoia,” had already made the same central distinction: Plan R* was designed to survive machines disappearing, while the sanctions regime targets banking, donations, domains, bandwidth, providers, and other surrounding relationships.

Sabot source preserved publicly at:

## Reporting significance

This predates the Sept. 5 public posts in which Shideler and Andy Ngo named Sabot directly. It shows that Shideler was already reading and using Sabot-produced material in his own public argument about A/I before later characterizing Sabot itself as part of a campaign “in support of” A/I.

The evidentiary point is limited: the article establishes use of the graphic and closely corresponding Sabot material. It does not by itself establish where Shideler first encountered the graphic, whether he knew its source at publication time, or whether the omission of attribution was intentional.
